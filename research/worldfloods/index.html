What Does the Clear–Cloudy Gap Measure? A Controlled Audit of WorldFloods Flood Segmentation

Kanak Anil Malpani. CVPR 2027 submission. Under review. The review manuscript’s title drops “WorldFloods” and ends at “A Controlled Audit of Flood Segmentation.”

Abstract

A clear–cloudy water-IoU gap mixes composition with a same-tile fill unless the composition control, the same-tile control, and the aggregator are named. We score released checkpoints under those three controls and do not train them. On WorldFloods the frozen UNet unmatched scene-mean gap is 39.3 [22.4, 57.6], water-fraction matching yields 8.9 [−0.7, 17.8], and water-fraction-plus-NDVI matching yields 1.4 [−24.5, 24.5]. The hard paste is 5.6 [3.0, 8.5] on 32 scenes, and the test scene mean is 3.8 [1.5, 6.9]. That paste is a constructed fill on clear tiles, scored on unpainted pixels. It is not natural cloud.

On C2S-MS Floods the same controls, applied to frozen Prithvi, give composition gaps that exclude zero: unmatched 27.8 [15.3, 39.0], water-fraction 21.1 [10.9, 32.2], and NDVI 25.4 [13.9, 36.1], each on 16 events, while the same-tile paired contrast includes zero. The same three controls show this separation on both datasets, so it is not a result of one checkpoint or one dataset. A larger corpus is informative only when it reports the same three controls, composition, same-tile, and aggregator; the two datasets show the controls, not a size effect. A third collection was inconclusive and is omitted.
