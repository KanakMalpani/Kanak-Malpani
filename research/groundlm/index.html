Efficient Adaptation of Small Language Models for Reliable Mathematical Reasoning

Kanak Anil Malpani. GroundLM Workshop, ACL 2026. Pages 1221–1231. Published.

Abstract

Efficient training and adaptation are central to deploying language models under resource constraints. We present a controlled comparison of six parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods—LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA, PiSSA, LoftQ, and AdaLoRA—across the full cross-product of three base models (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B) and two mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH), with every configuration trained on MetaMathQA under one identical budget and reported as mean ± standard deviation over three seeds. Qwen2.5-1.5B + PiSSA reaches 71.40 ± 0.27% on GSM8K, exceeding the unadapted 7B base model (68.50 ± 0.42%; Welch’s t(3.4) = 10.06, p = 0.001).

Significance testing shows that the method ordering—PiSSA > DoRA > LoRA ≈ AdaLoRA ≈ LoftQ ≈ QLoRA—is identical across all three base models (Kendall’s τ = 1.0), but that only the PiSSA and DoRA gains over LoRA survive multiple-comparison correction: the LoRA/QLoRA/LoftQ/AdaLoRA cluster is statistically indistinguishable at n = 3. Four-bit QLoRA cuts peak training VRAM by up to 69.6% (72.8% with PagedAdamW), within one point of the 68.7% predicted by a static-memory model, while PagedAdamW trades 15–20% throughput for memory safety. We add rank and target-module ablations, a GSM8K error taxonomy, and the finding that PiSSA’s spectral initialization at r = 16 outperforms LoRA at r = 64, and we release the training, evaluation, and profiling code with the exact configuration for every run.
