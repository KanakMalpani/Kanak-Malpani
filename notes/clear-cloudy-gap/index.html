The matched gap does not support a cloud effect

November 2026. Clear–cloudy flood segmentation. Under review.

A clear–cloudy water-IoU gap mixes two things unless you name them apart: the scenes were different pictures, and cloud filled the same tile. This note is the short version of that split. The manuscript is under review for CVPR 2027. No new model is trained for it. The scores are on released checkpoints.

Three controls have to be named: composition, same-tile, and the aggregator. On WorldFloods, a frozen UNet, the unmatched scene-mean gap is 39.3 points, interval [22.4, 57.6]. Matching on water fraction brings it to 8.9 [−0.7, 17.8]. Matching on water fraction and NDVI brings it to 1.4 [−24.5, 24.5]. The matched intervals include zero.

The same-tile check pastes a constructed fill onto clear tiles and scores the pixels that stay visible. That hard paste is 5.6 points [3.0, 8.5] on 32 scenes. The test scene mean is 3.8 [1.5, 6.9]. The paste is not natural cloud.

The same three controls on C2S-MS Floods, with frozen Prithvi, still separate the two stories. Composition gaps there exclude zero: unmatched 27.8 [15.3, 39.0], water-fraction 21.1 [10.9, 32.2], and NDVI 25.4 [13.9, 36.1], each on 16 events. The same-tile paired contrast includes zero. The split is not one checkpoint and not one dataset. A third collection was inconclusive and is left out. A larger corpus only helps if it reports the same three controls.
